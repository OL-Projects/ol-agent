The loop is the product.

Every agent can produce a paragraph. Very few can tell you whether the paragraph was right. OL's architecture is built around one question: what would prove this actually worked?

01 · Derive

It finds its own work

Goals are derived from live evidence — failing jobs, stalled units, drifted config, unpaid invoices — not from a prompt. When nobody is watching, it still has a queue.

02 · Execute

One unit, one agent

A bounded task runs on the cheapest model that can do it, with a wall-clock cap and a spend cap. Big work is split rather than attempted once.

03 · Verify

The doer never grades itself

A separate verifier re-runs the check the task declared. The agent's own claim of success has no weight — only the measurement does.

04 · Close

Evidence or it reopens

A closed unit carries its proof: a command, a hash, a row, a rendered page. Claims older than ninety minutes return to the queue by themselves.

05 · Learn

Failures become rules

A failure is recorded with its class, so the same mistake is not re-learned in the next session. The ledger is the memory.

06 · Survive

It comes back on its own

After a crash, a restart or a reboot it re-arms itself and resumes. A watchdog proves the recovery path by killing the app on purpose.

One harness, every model that earns its place.

OL is model-agnostic by design. The routing is a decision, not a subscription: the hardest judgement goes to the strongest rail, the mechanical work goes to the cheapest that passes.

  • →
    Fleet routing. Design, review and grading run on frontier models; plumbing runs on fast ones. A refusal on one rail retries on another instead of stopping.
  • →
    Bring your own keys. Your provider accounts, your spend. Nothing is marked up and nothing is proxied through us.
  • →
    Bounded spend. Per-tick and per-day ceilings, measured from the billing ledger, not from an estimate.
  • →
    Parallel where it is disjoint. Independent work fans out; anything that installs runs alone, in order.
  • →
    Capacity, not a declared number. Concurrency is sized from real memory and provider limits — the fleet is honest about how wide it can actually go.
  • →
    Kill switch. One command stops every loop, and the stop is verified rather than assumed.

What it cannot do

A harness that cannot name its limits has not been tested. This list is shorter than most products' feature lists, and it is the honest one.

Reality

It cannot verify what it cannot measure

If a task has no checkable outcome, OL will tell you it is unverified rather than guess. That is a feature with a cost: some work stays "probably fine".

Reality

It cannot outrun your model provider

Your keys, your limits. When your provider is down or out of credit, so is OL — it will not silently reroute your work through someone else's account.

Reality

It cannot be trusted with irreversible money

Payments, signatures and legal identity stay with a human. The harness is built to prepare those, not to perform them.
